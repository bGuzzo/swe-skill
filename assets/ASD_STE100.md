# ASD-STE100 Guide

## Source
Use ASD-STE100 Issue 9, dated January 15, 2025.
This file gives project guidance. It does not replace the complete standard or its dictionary.
The rules below cover the nine sections. They are not a copy of the standard.

* [Official standard: rules and dictionary](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf)
* [Official download page](https://asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html)
* [Official guidance on checking tools](https://asd-ste100.org/STEsoftware.html)

Use Part 1 for the complete rules and exceptions. Use Part 2 to check each general word.
Do not claim ASD approval or certification for this guide or an automatic check.

## 1. Words
* Check each general word against the dictionary. Check its meaning, part of speech, and permitted forms.
* Use a technical noun or verb outside the dictionary only if its category permits it.
* Use terms from the applicable company, industry, or subject glossary. Select short, clear terms.
* Keep one term for each item. Do not use slang or regional terms.
* Do not use a technical noun as a verb, or a technical verb as a noun.
* Prefer a dictionary verb when it gives the correct meaning.
* Use US spelling unless an official directive requires other spelling.
* For software terms, check the computer terminology categories in rules 1.5 and 1.12.
* A familiar software word is not automatically an approved term.

## 2. Noun Groups
* Keep noun groups to three words or fewer.
* Give a longer official technical noun in full first. Then define a shorter form if possible.
* Use hyphens to show related word groups when necessary. Do not change an official name.

## 3. Verbs
* Use approved verb forms. Use commands, infinitives, or simple present, past, and future tenses.
* Use past participles as adjectives. Do not use perfect or progressive tenses.
* Use an `-ing` form only as a technical noun or its modifier.
* Use active voice. In descriptions, use passive voice only if the actor is unknown.
* Express an action with a verb. Avoid complex auxiliary verb groups.

## 4. Sentences
* Give each sentence a clear structure. Keep all necessary words.
* Do not use contractions. Do not omit articles to reduce length.
* Use lists for complex information. Connect related sentences with clear connecting words.
* Before nouns, use articles or words such as `this` and `these` where applicable.

## 5. Instructions
* Limit each instruction sentence to 20 words.
* Give one instruction per sentence. Combine actions only if they occur at the same time.
* Use the command form. Put a necessary condition before the action.
* Keep steps in the order of work. Put instructions in steps, not in notes.
* Notes give information. Their sentences can have up to 25 words.

## 6. Descriptions
* Limit each descriptive sentence to 25 words.
* Give information in a logical order. Use consistent key terms to connect it.
* Give each paragraph one topic. State that topic first.
* Limit each paragraph to six sentences.

## 7. Safety Text
* Use the applicable safety label, such as `WARNING` or `CAUTION`.
* Start with the command or the condition that the reader needs.
* State the hazard and its possible result. Give a clear action to prevent it.
* Keep safety text before the applicable action. Do not put safety instructions in notes.

## 8. Punctuation and Word Counts
* Do not use semicolons in prose. Keep required code punctuation unchanged.
* Use hyphens for words that form one unit.
* Use parentheses only for the purposes in rule 8.3, such as references, identifiers, abbreviations, explanations, or alternatives.
* At a list colon, count the introduction and each item separately.
* Count a parenthetical group as one word. Count its contents separately against the sentence limit.
* Count each number, number with its unit, abbreviation, identifier, quotation, title, label, or proper name as one word.
* Count each hyphenated group as one word. Do not add hyphens to avoid a length limit.
* Use rules 8.4 through 8.7 for exact counts and exceptions.

## 9. Style
* Change the sentence structure if a word replacement does not give correct STE.
* Keep the original technical meaning. Use each approved word correctly.
* Do not create phrasal verbs. Check established dictionary verbs and permitted technical verbs before use.
* Keep terminology and sentence patterns consistent.

## Review
1. Separate prose from exact literals and quotations.
2. Check general words in Part 2. Check technical terms against Part 1 and the applicable glossary.
3. Check the meaning and part of speech of each word.
4. Check verbs, sentence structure, lengths, paragraphs, and punctuation.
5. Check instructions, conditions, notes, and safety text.
6. Compare the result with the source. Keep all required facts and actions.
7. Correct violations before delivery. State any limit on verification.

An automatic checker can help. It cannot prove that the meaning or all rules are correct.
Full STE compliance requires the complete rules, dictionary, and a review of the text.
